The honest picture. The channel-partner business can't fund this. By day 100 it should have earned ₹18–27 L of commission, and most of that is collected in Feb–Apr. The ₹2.5 Cr for December must come from outside the sales business: your own funds, a loan, a renegotiated schedule, or a project-level partner. Start on it this week, not in December.

RERA rules that shape the funding (confirm with your CA / lawyer)

  • No advertising or booking before RERA registration of the new project (for land over 500 m² or more than 8 flats). The Marketing Studio blocks posters for projects without a RERA number.
  • After registration, 70% of buyer payments go to the project's RERA account, usable only for that project's construction and land cost. So pre-sales can help the February land payment, and only if registration comes through by about mid-January.
  • Advance capped at 10% of the flat cost before a registered agreement for sale.
  • Money in one project's RERA account can't pay for another project. If you also have an ongoing project with unsold flats, its collections help this one only from the money you're allowed to withdraw.

Ways to cover ₹2.5 Cr by December (keeping 100% of the company)

  1. Renegotiate early (week 2): split the instalment (part in Dec, rest when plan approval or RERA comes), or give the landowner part of the value as built-up area / flats (joint-development style) instead of cash. Get any change in a written, properly executed supplementary agreement.
  2. Loan against property you or the firm own: usually 3–6 weeks, so apply in week 1.
  3. Documented private loans (family, friends, business contacts) at a fixed interest rate and term, with no shares in the company.
  4. Project-level partner: an investor funds this project for a share of this project's profit only.
  5. NBFC land or project finance (banks rarely fund land purchase by developers): compare the true cost carefully, as rates are high.

Avoid: unregulated money-lenders, open-ended personal guarantees, and selling shares in the main company.

Facts I need to build the detailed Project & Cash Plan

  • Project: location, land area, number of flats and sizes, expected prices
  • Deal: outright purchase or joint development; total land cost; schedule; penalty clauses
  • Approvals: planning-permission status; RERA registration status or expected date
  • Money: cash available now, other assets that could secure a loan, existing loans
  • Construction: contractor, total budget, stage-wise schedule
  • Any other ongoing project with unsold flats (the fastest sales cash, within RERA rules)

The 12-point CP agreement checklist

  1. Commission % and volume slabs in writing (e.g. 2% base → 2.5% after 3 bookings/quarter → 3% after 6)
  2. Basis: % of what? Basic price or agreement value; excluding GST, registration, maintenance, car park?
  3. Payout trigger and days (often: agreement registered + 10–20% collected; payment within 30–45 days)
  4. Client tagging: how (email/portal), ACK within 24 h, validity 60–90 days
  5. Disputes: first registered CP wins; a buyer walking in directly within validity is still ours
  6. Cancellation / clawback: only if cancelled before payout; no clawback after
  7. Spot incentives and offers passed to us in writing
  8. Price discipline: the builder's team won't undercut a buyer we registered
  9. Site support: site staff for our visits, sample flat, brochures, floor plans, price sheets
  10. Invoicing: GST 18% on commission; TDS under Sec. 194H (confirm the current rate with your CA); NEFT
  11. Buyer data used only for registration; no poaching of our buyers for other projects
  12. Term, termination, jurisdiction (Chennai / Bengaluru courts)
Red flags, walk away: fee or deposit for empanelment · project not RERA-registered · stalled site · reports of delayed CP payouts · builder wants buyer details before agreeing to protect your registration · "commission only after the full payment".

9. How to approach builders

Ways in, best first

  1. Warm introduction: bankers (home-loan managers know every builder), architects, other CPs, friends in the industry. Best response rate.
  2. Walk into the project sales office and ask for the CP manager. Weekday mornings are quiet; weekends are busy.
  3. CP meets and launch events: builders invite CPs at launches. Get added to each builder's CP WhatsApp group.
  4. CREDAI chapters and property expos in Chennai and Bengaluru: meet many builders in one day.
  5. LinkedIn to Sales Heads / VP Sales, short message + one-pager.
  6. Cold call the CP desk numbers listed on websites and hoardings.
