Skyline Realty — 15-Year Plan (2027–2041)

From an AI-powered channel partner to a ₹8,800 Cr (~$1B) real-estate company, 100% owned by the two partners · November 2026

1. The goal

By December 2041: a company worth about ₹8,800 Cr (~$1B at ₹88/$), owned 100% by the two partners. At about 15× profit (a normal value for a profitable, growing developer) that means about ₹590 Cr profit a year, from about ₹17,000 Cr of homes sold a year across sales and development.

Why 15 years, not 10. Keeping 100% means growing only on profits, borrowing and investor money inside individual projects, never by selling shares in the company. The capital model (section 4) shows this reaches about $1B in 15 years, not 10. The cautious case still reaches about ₹3,389 Cr (~$385M). Every stage is designed to be profitable on its own, so missing the top target still leaves a large business that you own completely.

₹7 Cr becomes about ₹14.5 Cr. Per year: 27% if the money returns in 36 months, 23% in 42 months, 20% in 48 months. Approval speed and pre-sales decide the return.

The rules that lift the return to ~24% a year

  1. Land ≤ 20% of sales value, bought where the demand book shows buyers waiting.
  2. JV or deferred land payments (instalments linked to approvals and sales) whenever possible.
  3. Plotted layouts (approved plots in Chennai/Bangalore growth corridors) for about 2-year cycles.
  4. Launch within weeks of RERA approval; about 40% sold in 6 months, so buyers fund construction.
  5. Never more than about 60% of your capital in one project, and keep the ₹1 Cr reserve.

Finance facts: banks generally can't fund land purchase by developers. Land loans come from NBFCs or housing finance companies at about 14–18%, or a loan against property you own. Banks fund construction after approvals (about 10–12%). Confirm terms with your banker and CA.

6. Investor capital in project companies: how it works

Structure

  • Skyline Realty Pvt Ltd (holding company): 100% you and your wife. It owns the brand, the sales engine, the platform and Skyline's share of every project.
  • One company per project: Skyline puts in 20–30% of the equity; investors put in 70–80%.
  • Typical terms: investors get their money back first plus an agreed return (about 12–14% a year). Above that, profits are shared, with Skyline getting a larger share (often 20–30% extra, the "promote"). Skyline also earns a development management fee (about 3–5% of sales).
  • Later (around 2037): a SEBI-registered alternative investment fund (AIF) run with a licensed partner, so larger investors can join.

The order to build trust

  1. 2027–28: deliver your current JV project well and record every number.
  2. 2029: first ₹10–15 Cr from people who know you (with written agreements, lawyer-drafted terms and proper compliance).
  3. 2031: first own-capital project delivered on time: a full track record.
  4. 2032–36: HNIs, family offices, NBFC partnerships; quarterly investor reports from the dashboard.
  5. 2037+: the fund vehicle; institutional co-investors.

Raising money from investors is regulated (company law, SEBI rules on public offers and funds). Take legal advice before every raise. Never advertise for investors without it.

What we will not do

  • Sell shares in the main company (the reason for 15 years)
  • Put all capital into one site, or buy land without the demand data
  • Borrow from unregulated lenders or give open-ended personal guarantees
  • Launch before RERA registration or take more than 10% before a registered agreement
  • Raise investor money without lawyer-drafted, compliant terms

The one-line strategy

Win buyers more cheaply than anyone with AI. Use that demand to build your own projects fast and safely. Then let investors fund projects, not the company. Grow only through gates you have proven, and never sell a share.
